# Ed Agent — Validation Interview Guide

Two guides: one for teachers, one for students. Aim for 15–20 minutes each.
Run 5–8 teacher interviews and 5–8 student interviews before writing more code
than you already have. You're not selling the product yet — you're testing
whether the problems in your blueprint are real and ranked correctly.

**Rules for the conversation:**
- Ask about their current behavior and pain, not their opinion of your idea. "How do you plan a lesson right now?" beats "Would you use an AI lesson planner?"
- Don't describe Ed Agent until Part 2. Let them describe the problem first, unprompted.
- Write down their exact words for pain points — you'll reuse this language in your marketing and onboarding copy later.
- If they say "that sounds cool" without a specific behavior change, that's not validation. Push for "would you actually switch from what you do now, and why."

---

## Part A — Teacher Interview

### 1. Warm-up / context (2 min)
- What grades and subjects do you teach? Class sizes?
- How many years teaching? What tools do you currently use to plan/grade (WhatsApp, Excel, printed books, nothing)?

### 2. Problem discovery — lesson planning (4 min)
- Walk me through how you planned your last lesson, start to finish.
- How long did it take? What's the most tedious part?
- Where do you go when you're unsure how to explain a concept, or need examples?

### 3. Problem discovery — assessment & grading (4 min)
- How do you currently create quizzes/exams? Where do questions come from?
- How do you check whether a question is actually good (right difficulty, aligned to the syllabus)?
- How much time per week goes to grading? What's hardest to grade fairly (memorized answers vs actual reasoning)?

### 4. Problem discovery — feedback & individualization (3 min)
- With [X] students per class, how do you know who's actually struggling with a concept vs who's behind on the final answer only?
- Do you get any signal on which of your own questions/lessons worked well or badly?

### 5. Reaction to the concept (4 min)
*Now describe Ed Agent in one sentence: "An AI tool that helps you plan lessons, generate and check exam questions, and grade student reasoning — while you stay in control and can edit or override anything it produces."*
- Gut reaction?
- Which part would save you the most time?
- What would make you NOT trust it / not use it?
- Would you want it to just do the work, or coach you through doing it faster?

### 6. Prototype walkthrough (if showing it)
- Hand them the prototype, give a task ("plan a lesson on X" or "generate 5 questions on Y"), watch without helping.
- Where did they hesitate or get confused?
- What did they expect to happen that didn't?
- Would they trust the output enough to use it with edits, or fully rewrite it?

### 7. Wrap-up
- If this existed today, free, would you use it weekly? What would stop you?
- Who else should I talk to (head of department, other teachers)?

## Part B — Student Interview

### 1. Warm-up / context (2 min)
- Grade, subjects, how do you usually study for math/physics?
- Do you get tutoring outside school? Use any apps already?

### 2. Problem discovery (5 min)
- Think of the last time you were stuck on a math/physics problem. What did you do?
- When a teacher explains something and you still don't get it, what happens next?
- Do you feel like you understand *why* something works, or mostly just how to get the answer for the test?
- What's the last thing you learned that connected to something you actually care about (a game, sport, career)?

### 3. Reaction to the concept (4 min)
*Describe: "An AI tutor that doesn't just give you the answer — it asks you questions to help you figure it out yourself, and can connect the topic to things you're interested in."*
- Would that be more or less annoying than just getting the answer? Be honest.
- What would make you actually open this instead of asking a friend or searching Google/YouTube?

### 4. Prototype walkthrough
- Give them a real problem from their own homework/textbook, let them try the tutor flow.
- Did the Socratic questioning feel helpful or frustrating?
- At what point would they want to just be told the answer?

### 5. Wrap-up
- Would you use this instead of / alongside tuition or YouTube?
- What would your parents think about you using it?

---

## After each round of interviews
- Tag every pain point by frequency (how many people said it unprompted).
- Compare against the blueprint's "Core Problems to Solve" — which are confirmed, which are weaker than assumed, which are missing entirely.
- Only after this: lock the MVP scope (blueprint Section 12) and start Phase 1.
